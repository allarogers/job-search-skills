# Write like a person

Detectors and readers react to the same thing: text that is predictable and uniform. Predictable word choice, even sentence rhythm, stock phrases and templated rhetoric are what give AI writing away. The fix is to write with specifics and variety, in the user's voice. Do not chase a detector score, and do not add typos or odd phrasing to fake human error. That makes the writing worse and doesn't fix the underlying pattern.

## Rules

1. **Be specific.** Replace generic claims with a concrete detail, number, name or example. If none exists, cut the sentence. Generic statements are the most predictable text there is.
2. **Vary rhythm.** Mix short and long sentences. Don't run three sentences in a row with the same length or structure, and vary how paragraphs open. Uniform rhythm is one of the main statistical signals.
3. **Cut stock phrases and intensifiers.** Examples: "in today's digital landscape", "ever-evolving", "in conclusion", "crucial", "significant", "game-changing", "revolutionary". These recur across millions of outputs. The list is illustrative: cut anything that sounds like filler, not just these.
4. **Use plain transitions or none.** Avoid "furthermore", "moreover" and "additionally". Drop any transition that carries no meaning. Swapping one connector for a synonym doesn't change the pattern, so restructure instead.
5. **Skip templated rhetoric.** That means "It's not X, it's Y", "Here's the thing:", "Here's the uncomfortable truth:", forced lists of three, and fragments used only for emphasis. They announce importance without earning it.
6. **Commit to claims.** Hedge only where the uncertainty is real, and say what it is. Blanket hedging reads as machine caution.
7. **Match the voice.** Use contractions and the register of the user's own samples if they gave any. No flattery or sycophancy.
8. **Go light on em dashes.** At most one per page. Prefer a period or comma.
9. **Avoid repetition in a short span.** Don't reuse the same word or phrase close together. Rephrase naturally instead of thesaurus-swapping.
10. **Never invent.** Don't make up anecdotes, experiences or facts the user didn't give. If a paragraph needs a concrete detail you don't have, ask for it.

## Example

Before: "In today's fast-paced world, effective communication is crucial. Furthermore, it's not just about talking, it's about listening."

After: "Most meeting problems I see come down to people waiting for their turn to talk." (Only if the user supplied that observation. Otherwise ask what they've actually seen.)

## Check before returning

- Search the draft for every phrase in rules 3 to 5.
- Compare sentence lengths within each paragraph and fix any run of similar ones.
- Confirm each paragraph has at least one concrete detail.
- Check every list of three items and every paragraph opener for repetition.
- Return only the text unless the user asks for an explanation. If they asked for several versions, give the versions without commentary.
